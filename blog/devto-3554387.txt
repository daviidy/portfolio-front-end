14:T1b09,<p>I'm going to be honest with you.</p>
<p>Most engineers using AI assistants today are shipping at the same speed as before.</p>
<p>They have Cursor. They have Claude Code. They watched the demos, saw the vibe coding videos, and said, "<em>This is going to change everything</em>." And nothing changed.</p>
<p>Here's the thing nobody wants to say out loud: <strong>LLMs are amplifiers. Not magic.</strong></p>
<p>That's it. That's the whole thesis. An amplifier takes whatever signal you give it and makes it louder. If you plug in a clean signal, you get a clean sound. If you plug in noise, you get louder noise.</p>
<p>Most engineers are plugging in noise.</p>
<p>They open a chat window with zero context. No specs. No planning. No clear definition of what they're building. They ask the model to write a feature; it generates something; they copy it; it breaks; they debug for two hours; they paste the error back in; it generates a fix that breaks something else. And they call that AI-assisted development.</p>
<p>That's not development. That's chaos at 10x speed.</p>
<p>Here's what's really happening in this AI era. You're no longer a developer. You're the product manager, the architect, the QA engineer, AND the developer, all at once.</p>
<p>But if you don't know the principles of software projects, you're just equipped with a tool that mirrors your disorganization back at you confidently and at scale.</p>
<p>You didn't get slower because AI is bad. You got slower because you skipped the step that makes AI powerful: <strong>the spec</strong>.</p>
<p>Now, let me be clear — you're not alone in this. I'm not saying you're incompetent.</p>
<p>Some of the best engineers I've seen fall into this trap. And there are workflows out there built specifically to solve it.</p>
<p><a href="https://github.com/bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD?tab=readme-ov-file">BMAD</a>. <a href="https://github.com/addyosmani/agent-skills/tree/main">Agent Skills</a>. Multiple frameworks built by engineers with real experience, engineers who lead real projects</p>
<p>They're not geniuses</p>
<p>Just people who mastered the principles of project management before they touched a model.</p>
<p>These workflows exist because the people who built them understood one thing: <strong>you don't pick up tools before you have a plan.</strong></p>
<p>I want to share my version. Four phases that are similar to the well-known Spec-Driven Development with LLMs.</p>
<p>Every artifact across all four phases is stored in a Markdown file. That's not an accident — markdown keeps your workflow portable, readable, and model-agnostic. Whatever model is trending next month, your specs still work.</p>
<hr>
<h3>Phase 1 — Context</h3>
<p>This is where you feed the model everything before it writes a single line of code.</p>
<p>Epics. User stories. Story descriptions. Technical requirements. Stack decisions. Non-negotiables.</p>
<p>Think of it as onboarding a new engineer to your team. You would never hand someone a laptop on day one and say, "Build the app." You walk them through the product. You explain the architecture. You tell them what they cannot touch. You set the context.</p>
<p>Do the same thing with your LLM.</p>
<p>No context = the model is going to hallucinate. And I've seen this happen. Engineers waste hours debugging code that was never wrong — it just solved a different problem than the one they had in their head.</p>
<hr>
<h3>Phase 2 — Planning</h3>
<p>Now the model has context. Time to make a plan.</p>
<p>You instruct the model to break each user story into small, achievable milestones. Not one big chunk. Not a wall of code. A sequenced plan with clear checkpoints. And you save that plan to a markdown file.</p>
<p>This step alone separates engineers who are faster with AI from engineers who aren't.</p>
<p>Why? Because planning forces clarity. It forces you to think five steps ahead instead of just reacting to whatever the model generates.</p>
<p>You know what the difference between an engineer who stagnates and one who evolves is? One of them attacks their work with intention. The other just reacts.</p>
<p>The planning phase is where you build intention into your workflow.</p>
<hr>
<h3>Phase 3 — Building</h3>
<p>This is where the actual development happens. And there is one rule. One non-negotiable.</p>
<p><strong>The model does not move to the next step without your approval.</strong></p>
<p>Every milestone. You review it before the model continues.</p>
<p>I know what you're thinking — that sounds slow. It's not. It's the opposite. Because the engineers who skip this step are the ones ending up with 3,000 lines of AI-generated code they don't understand and cannot debug. Then they spend three days trying to reverse engineer what the model was built for. That's not fast. That's a disaster.</p>
<p>Approval gates keep you in the loop. And in this phase, you can also bring in a second model as a code reviewer. You can use a skill to generate PRs. You can run automated checks.</p>
<p>The workflow becomes a system, and a system is what separates someone who scales from someone who grinds.</p>
<hr>
<h3>Phase 4 — Learning</h3>
<p>This is the phase almost nobody talks about. And it's the most powerful one.</p>
<p>When you encounter a bug, for example, and you fix it, the model should update its memory — its instructions, its specs — so that pattern never happens again. If you find a fix, the model documents it. If you discover a constraint specific to your stack, it gets added to the context for the next session.</p>
<p>Phase 4 turns your AI assistant from a stateless code generator into something that actually learns how you work. Your conventions. Your edge cases, etc.</p>
<p>Most engineers use AI like a calculator. They pick it up, punch in a problem, get an answer, and put it down. They don't build on it. They don't make their codebase smarter over time.</p>
<p>That's why their shipping speed hasn't changed. The tool never learned them.</p>
<p>There are many AI workflows available right now — BMAD, Agent Skills, and others — built by engineers who've shipped real products. They're worth studying. Use them.</p>
<p>But before you pick one, understand the four principles underneath all of them:</p>
<p><strong>Context → Planning → Building with approval gates → Learning.</strong></p>
<p>Master those, and it won't matter which model is trending, which tool drops next week, or what the next big demo on X looks like.</p>
<p>LLMs are amplifiers. Feed them a solid spec — they'll amplify your output. Feed them chaos — and they'll amplify that too.</p>
<p>The choice is yours.</p>
